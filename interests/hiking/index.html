Chapter 1

Why I hike

It's the one place my phone stays in my pocket. A long climb leaves room to think through a problem or not think at all, and reaching the top always feels earned.

Keep reading

I like that every trail is a little different, and that the same trail is never the same twice. A loop I've done a dozen times looks completely new in fog, at sunset or after a rainy week, when everything is green and the creeks are actually running.

Chapter 2

Favorite trails

The best trails are the ones that make you work for it: a steady climb, a few false summits, then a ridge where the whole Bay opens up below.

  • Short and steep: a fast climb to a viewpoint after class
  • Long and rolling: a full day out with friends and a packed lunch
  • Sunrise or golden hour: worth the early alarm or the walk down in the dark
Keep reading

Close to campus, the East Bay hills are perfect for a quick loop before or after class. On longer weekends I go looking for anything with real elevation gain and a view at the end: coastal bluffs, redwood groves, or a peak where you can see three counties at once.

Chapter 3

How I plan a hike

Most of the planning is about the boring stuff that makes the fun part possible: how long it is, how much climbing, where the water is, and when the sun goes down. I check the weather and the trail conditions before I leave, and I always tell someone where I'm going if I'm heading out alone.

Keep reading

Distance is easy to underestimate and elevation is easy to ignore, so I budget time by both: half an hour per mile plus half an hour per thousand feet of climbing (the trail planner below does the math). Then I set a turnaround time and stick to it, even if the summit is close.

Chapter 4

What's in my pack

I like to keep it light, but there are a few things that always come with me, no matter how short the hike looks on the map.

  • More water than I think I'll need
  • A snack for the top (the view tastes better with one)
  • A light layer: the ridge is always windier than the trailhead
  • Sunscreen, a hat and a charged phone with an offline map
  • A small first-aid kit, just in case

Chapter 5

Hiking with friends vs. solo

Hiking with friends turns a climb into a long conversation. Some of my best talks have happened halfway up a switchback, when everyone is too out of breath to be anything but honest.

Keep reading

Solo hikes are different: quieter, faster, and the best way I know to untangle a problem. I've worked out more bugs on a trail than at a desk. Whichever kind it is, I leave the trail how I found it: pack out what I pack in, stay on the path, and give way to people coming uphill.

Chapter 6

Still to climb

The list of trails I want to do keeps getting longer. Next up: more long-distance day hikes, a first proper overnight backpacking trip, and more of the big national parks a few hours' drive from the Bay.

Keep reading

Eventually I want to stand on top of a real mountain, the kind where you start before sunrise and the trail ends above the tree line.

Appendix · field notebook

Trail planner

How long will it take? Hikers use 'book time': half an hour per mile plus half an hour per 1,000 feet of climbing. Plug in a trail and see when you'll be back and how much water to bring.
